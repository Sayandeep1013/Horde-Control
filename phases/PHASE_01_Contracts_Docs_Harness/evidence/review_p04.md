## 8. Score and reasons

**7 / 10.** Below the bar of 8, so the reasons are stated.

What is genuinely strong, and should not be lost in the findings: twenty Register citations, twenty resolving to real rows in the correct sections, with quoted row titles matching character for character, and the single most demanding one (A2's two tuning targets) landing on a row that carries both. Document Control compliance is clean on every check - zero placeholders, no gate or stable claim, header shape matching docs/28 exactly, versions at 1.0.0. The author's gate row was not written and the master's only diff is four decision rows. Doc 02's six Owns entries each resolve to a section that does real work. Two gaps were self-reported honestly (F01-06, F01-07) rather than smoothed over, and doc 01 names an unresolved pillar conflict as an open question instead of inventing a resolution, which is what the phase's carried lesson 4 asked for.

The three reasons it does not reach 8:

1. **R1.** A rule that does not exist in CLAUDE.md is cited three times, in the implementer's own words, as the authority for four exemptions from the task's single highest-risk rule. It did not reach the artifacts, and three of the four exemptions survive on the master's wording anyway - but the project has shipped this exact defect before, the phase wrote a carried lesson about it, and it recurred in the evidence for the task most exposed to it. A reviewer trusting the report would have inherited it.
2. **R7.** Doc 01 declares a six-rank precedence order binding on the project, and an anti-pattern-to-pillar mapping the master does not make, without a Review Decision Log row naming the alternative. CLAUDE.md requires the row. Whether the remit exempts it is the author's call, but the record does not currently let the author make that call.
3. **R2.** The precedence table's rank 1 cannot fire, by the table's own precondition, in a document whose entire purpose is that table. Cheap to fix, but it is the deliverable.

R3 through R6 are fidelity slips of a kind a spot-check expects to find; individually none would move the score.
